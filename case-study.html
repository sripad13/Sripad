Beyond the Rumour: TheSportzine

Building a football publication around credibility, original sourcing and contextual storytelling—rather than simply repeating the transfer rumour of the day.

The challenge

Football fans are surrounded by transfer rumours, anonymous insiders and speculation that can spread across social media before anyone establishes whether a story is credible.

I saw a growing problem with this information cycle. Unreliable accounts on X (formerly Twitter) were presenting speculation as inside information, while reports from obscure websites could be repeated by larger newsrooms without enough scrutiny. For fans, distinguishing a genuine development from an invented claim was becoming increasingly difficult.

I wanted TheSportzine to address that problem. Rather than simply rewriting reports or racing to publish every rumour, I wanted to build a football publication that investigated the context behind important stories, connected developments over time and helped readers understand what might actually be happening.

The strategy: Context before noise

01 · Build reporting around relationships

Having worked in football journalism since the early 2010s, I had developed contacts across the industry, including people connected to clubs and player representation. I used those relationships to understand developments beyond the public narrative, gather context and learn how the people involved viewed a situation. Over time, I continued developing this network to reach more informed sources.

02 · Reconstruct the full story

Major football stories rarely emerge from a single conversation or report. I approached complex situations by combining source conversations, published reporting, timelines and analysis. The aim was to connect developments that might otherwise be treated as unrelated news updates, while filtering conflicting information and identifying the details that added the most value.

03 · Cross-check before publishing

Information from one source was not automatically treated as established fact. I cross-checked reports against other sources and looked for overlapping information to help assess which details were sufficiently supported. This informed editorial decisions about what to include and how to frame a story.

04 · Combine deep reporting with social-first publishing

Long-form reporting was one part of the strategy. The social media approach also included breaking news, exclusives, live match updates and quizzes. This mix gave the brand different ways to serve football fans: timely updates, deeper explanations and interactive content between major news events.

Execution

As founder and hands-on content strategist, I led the editorial direction and handled most of the editorial work myself.

  • Identified stories where additional reporting could offer more than a routine news rewrite.
  • Developed and maintained source relationships across football.
  • Researched developments, reconstructed timelines and cross-checked information.
  • Shaped articles and planned social content across breaking news, exclusives, live match updates and quizzes.

The central editorial decision was to prioritise stories where additional reporting could offer readers something beyond a routine news rewrite.

Results and impact

TheSportzine has built an X audience of more than 50,000 followers, according to the audience figure supplied for this case study.

I have also seen strong social media interest in selected stories, including the Enzo Fernández deep dive and the Salomon Kalou quotes article. Social media reports and audience comments have provided feedback on which stories resonate with readers.

The publication has developed a reputation for exclusives, reflecting the importance I place on source relationships and taking the time to investigate a story before publishing.

Measurement note

The follower figure is owner-reported. The exact growth period, post-level reach, engagement totals and website traffic have not been established here. The audience figure is not attributed entirely to one campaign or article.

What I learned

TheSportzine reinforced an important principle: a publication does not have to compete on speed alone if it can offer readers a clearer understanding of the story.

Building that proposition requires more than writing ability. It requires source development, editorial judgement, research discipline, a consistent content strategy and an understanding of how different formats serve an audience.

The takeaway

TheSportzine demonstrates my ability to define an editorial proposition, translate it into a working content strategy, develop reporting relationships, lead hands-on editorial execution and build an audience around a distinctive approach to football coverage.
